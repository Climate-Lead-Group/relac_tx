# Introduction

The RELAC initiative (Renewables in Latin America and the Caribbean) seeks to promote climate action by increasing the share of renewable energy in the region's power systems. Originally aiming for 70% renewable electricity generation across its member countries, the goal was later raised to 80% of total electricity generation and 73% of installed capacity by 2030.

In this context, the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) commissioned Climate Lead Group to carry out a prospective regional analysis of transmission needs to support RELAC's enhanced renewable energy targets. While previous studies confirmed the technical and economic feasibility of reaching these levels of renewable generation, they did not assess whether the planned transmission infrastructure could accommodate such growth.

This study addresses that gap by evaluating whether existing and planned transmission networks can effectively support the enhanced renewable and electrification targets. It further identifies the additional infrastructure needed, such as new lines, grid repowering and energy storage, to enable the secure and efficient integration of renewable generation. The findings aim to inform strategic decision-making by pinpointing investment priorities, resolving potential grid bottlenecks, and identifying opportunities to lower system costs and accelerate the energy transition.
